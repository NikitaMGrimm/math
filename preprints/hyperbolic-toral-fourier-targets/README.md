## Abstract

For every hyperbolic automorphism $A \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ of the two-torus and $\tau > 1$, we study the sets $D \subseteq P \subseteq V$ of centers of balls of radius $c\lambda^{-\tau n}$ hit infinitely often from a dense set of parameters ($D$), at least one parameter ($P$), or possibly varying witnesses ($V$) on a prescribed arc. All three share local Hausdorff dimension $1 + 1/\tau$ and Fourier dimension zero, yet optimal full-time Fourier decay splits three ways purely by arc geometry: $o(1/\log\log|\xi|)$ on the unstable eigenline, $o(1/\log|\xi|)$ on every mixed line, and $o((\log|\xi|)^{-3/2})$ on every regular $C^2$ arc with nonzero curvature somewhere in every interval (even at isolated eigentangencies), with a single compact $D$-supported probability attaining all subcritical energies and larger powers annihilating even $V$. A glued flat+curved embedded arc separates $D$ (power $1$) from $P, V$ (power $3/2$). We also establish sparse-time criteria, a totally real cubic Pisot $d = 3$ dichotomy, and a critical $\tau = 1$ center whose fibers on every transverse affine line simultaneously exclude a prescribed decay rate.
